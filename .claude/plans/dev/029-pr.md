## Worth a closer look
- **The claim has no end date.** It's per workflow and order, not "once per customer per 90 days", so a later order that goes quiet starts a new period by itself. That's why the release paths matter: `Dispatcher::dispatch` and `Worker::release_claim`.
- **Real statuses, one set for both sides** (`InactiveSweep::NOT_REAL`). Pending, failed, cancelled and checkout draft don't count. On-hold and refunded do, so a customer with a newer on-hold order gets no "we miss you".
- **Per-batch flush.** `InactiveSweep::batch` calls `Runtime::dispatcher()->flush()`, because a long Action Scheduler process has one shutdown for many batches.
- **A run doesn't stop when the customer orders during a Wait.** The v1.0 templates have no Wait; it's in the v1.1 backlog.
- **Known limits** are listed under Cost in `docs/workflow-core-decisions.md`, section M6. One to weigh: activating a second win-back version emails every customer still in the window again: claims are per workflow. The v1.1 backlog has the fix.

## How to test
- `./run.sh check-winback` (about 2.5 minutes; it creates and removes 500 orders). It stops if the site has an active Customer inactive workflow.
- Trigger picker → Customers shows Customer inactive. 6 and 731 show the range messages; 90.5 saves as 91.

- Run them again: no new run. An unsubscribed customer's run ends at the Condition's No path, 1 step, no coupon.
- Settings → Health check: cancel the job, reload, click Schedule again.
