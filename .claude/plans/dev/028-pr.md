## Worth a closer look
- `CustomerAccountCreated::claim()`: `woocommerce_created_customer` also fires for every saved `new WC_Customer`, so without the `woocommerce_new_customer` guard an ERP sync would email its whole list. The guard is per id, tested with a shopper and a saved customer in one request.
- `ReviewProducts::reviewed_ids()`: `get_comments()` ignores an empty `post__in` or `author_email`, and `user_id` 0 matches every guest. The guards stop any of them marking everything reviewed.
- The `flow.ts` refactor touches every cart and cross-sell template. All 12 existing graphs were dumped before and after: only the greeting line of the 4 cross-sell templates changed.

## Known limits
- High-value: processing → on-hold → processing or completed alerts again. A late payment on a cancelled order doesn't alert.
- An erase keeps the WordPress account, so the run label still shows its email until the user is deleted.
- Reviews: WordPress's "Users must be registered and logged in to comment" sends guests to a login. WooCommerce's "verified owners only" hides the form for buyers of a guest order. The `#reviews` anchor is checked on classic templates only.
- `wc.reviewsEnabled` stays stale until the page reloads.
- Welcome: bot sign-ups get welcome emails. An importer calling `wc_create_new_customer()` directly looks like a shopper and starts it. A sign-up outside WooCommerce (`wp-login.php`, a plugin calling `wp_insert_user()`) doesn't.
- A customer deleted during a wait: later emails are skipped and the run ends Completed.
- The second welcome email with no visible products has no grid. "New" means the store's newest, however old.
- `customer.full_name` on an account with no billing details is the first name only.
